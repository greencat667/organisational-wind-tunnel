## 2026-09-21 — sweep fixes, allocate() perf rewrite
- Fixed `active_members()` re-scanning `team.member_ids` on every read; cached per team, invalidated on hire/departure/team-move.
- Fixed Apple FM's `responsive()` probe: it tested plain chat, but guided JSON generation (what every real interpretation call uses) is the mode that actually hangs and can wedge the shared on-device queue afterward even though plain chat still answers. Live-verified this still happens today. Probe now uses a schema-guided request; a negative result is cached 300s instead of 60s.
- Implemented the `working/proposals/allocate-perf-fix.md` heap rewrite: one lazy-deletion min-heap per skill replaces the O(team_size) `min()` rescan on every work-chunk assignment (cProfile: 3.06M such scans in one month at 2,000 employees). Caught one bug during implementation — a newly-built skill heap must seed each worker's *current* load ratio, not 0.0, since a worker can already carry load from an earlier skill this call. Verified behaviour-preserving against 3,622 real (team, items, members) snapshots (byte-identical old vs new) plus a 36-month determinism re-run. `_allocate`'s share of a month's time at 2,000 employees: ~55% → ~13%; wall-clock at 10,000 employees: ~67s/month → ~6.5s/month. `_process_work` and `_psychology` are now the dominant cost at scale — a separate, unaddressed bottleneck.
- Fixed `_process_work` (same heap-per-skill technique) and `recent_team_causes`/`recent_emp_causes` (per-team/per-employee event index now bucketed by kind at `emit()` time, so a lookup for a handful of rare kinds no longer wades through the far more frequent `employee_overloaded` noise). Verified behaviour-preserving: shadow run of both old algorithms across both templates and six seeds, 30 months each — metrics_history, event descriptions, and event causes lists byte-identical in all 12 runs. Wall-clock at 10,000 employees: ~6.5s/month → ~1.3s/month. Still worse than linear: traced the remainder to `orggen.py` seeding an informal relationship between every pair of teammates with 45% probability, so relationships-per-person scale with team size, making `_psychology`'s informal-ties decay O(n²) — a modelling choice in org generation, not an implementation bug, so left alone pending a decision on whether to cap it.
- Christian: cap relationships per person at a fixed number regardless of team size — nobody realistically knows more than ~150 people. Added `SimConfig.max_relationships_per_person` (default 150, Dunbar's number), enforced everywhere a relationship can be created (org generation, both within-team and cross-team ties, plus the `seek_help` tie-strengthening action). Within-team seeding rewritten from an all-pairs Bernoulli(0.45) roll (O(team_size²), degree scaling with team size) to each person sampling a bounded number of teammates directly; halved the target-degree formula (0.45 → 0.225) since either side sampling the other roughly doubles realised degree versus one roll per pair, verified to keep the shipped templates' average density close to before (prototype 5.9 vs ~6.5; charity500 9.25 vs ~9.6) while a 10,000-employee org now holds flat at 150 instead of averaging ~650. 34 tests pass; determinism holds both templates, 36 months. `_psychology` per-employee cost now roughly flattens with scale (5.2µs at 1,000 employees → 19.0µs at 20,000, was → 120µs before); full step() at 10,000 employees ~1.3s/month → ~0.9s/month, 20,000 (previously impractical) now ~2.8s/month.
- Ran a 12-seed batch sweep at 10,000 employees to confirm outcomes hold: they didn't ("stable" 0%, vs ~50% at native scale). Root cause was a real bug, not miscalibration: `transfer_item()` taxes an item 25% whenever it lands on a team whose declared `skills_provided` doesn't cover its skill, but `seek_help`/`_manager_redistribute` gate transfers with the looser `team_can_do()` check (accepts a team if just two members have incidental proficiency ≥0.35) — every transfer that only qualifies that way triggers the penalty, uncapped. Only reachable at scale: too few items in flight at 100 employees for the unlucky streak to occur; near-certain at 10,000. Verified: same seed now stays bounded (backlog 0.06→0.28 over 36 months, was →3.17; worst team ≤0.89, was 17.9); no item across four seeds ever exceeds exactly 1.25² regardless of how long the run goes. 34 tests pass; determinism holds at both scales.
